Part 2 · Case Study
MGM Resorts, September 2023
▶

Scattered Spider + ALPHV/BlackCat · Voice phishing (vishing) attack

They didn't write malware or send a phishing email — they called IT support and simply logged in.

In September 2023, a hacker collective called Scattered Spider looked up an MGM employee's public LinkedIn profile to learn their name and department. Then they called the IT help desk, pretended to be that employee, claimed they had a new phone, and convinced the agent to reset MFA. The helpful IT support person did exactly that — and the attackers walked right through the front door.

Real Incidents

Arup — Hong Kong, Feb 2024

A finance worker at engineering firm Arup was invited to a Zoom call with the CFO and multiple senior executives to discuss an urgent payment. He wired $25 million. Every person on that Zoom call was an AI-generated deepfake — voices cloned from public conference videos, faces generated in real time. Nobody caught it.

✓
Ferrari — Italy, Jul 2024

A senior executive received WhatsApp messages from someone claiming to be CEO Benedetto Vigna, followed by a phone call with a perfect clone of the CEO's voice — including his specific southern Italian accent. The executive got suspicious and asked a personal question: "What was the title of the book you just recommended me?" The AI couldn't answer. The attacker hung up.

✗
Singapore — Mar 2025

Same deepfake Zoom pattern — but this time, the attackers proactively suggested the video call, knowing the target would feel reassured by "seeing" the executives. $499K transferred.

🏎 Ferrari caught it. Arup didn't. Singapore didn't. Should security depend on human judgment?

Clone any voice from 3 seconds of audio. Generate deepfake video in real time. Run 50 campaigns simultaneously. AI didn't invent social engineering — it eliminated every constraint that used to limit it.

A human attacker still drives the conversation — the AI generates the voice and face.

Part 4
Defending Against AI-Powered Social Engineering
▶

If human judgment isn't reliable — whether it's a help desk agent on the phone or a finance worker on Zoom — how do we defend?

1

Lean Into the Three Factors

Physical & local over remote
▶

AI can clone a face on a Zoom call, but it cannot clone a physical FIDO2 key sitting on your desk.

🧠 Blind Support PINs

Employee sets secret PIN at hire. Agent types it in → system returns Pass/Fail. Agent never knows the PIN. Can't social-engineer what the agent doesn't have.

📱 FIDO2 Hardware Keys

System asks "do you have the key?" — not "are you who you say you are?" The key generates a unique cryptographic challenge-response per service. The private key never leaves the device. Lost key = in-person replacement only. A deepfake can clone a face — it can't clone a cryptographic token.

👤 On-Device Biometrics

FaceID on the physical device — not a face on a Zoom call the attacker controls.

2

Policy-Based Controls

Quorum approval · manager sign-off · cool-off sandbox
▶
👥 Quorum / Manager Approval

Privileged reset → two senior staff sign off. Single help desk agent can't do it alone. MGM: one agent reset an Okta admin. With quorum — impossible.

⏳ Cool-Off Sandbox

After any MFA reset: email only for 12–24 hours. No admin consoles. Unconditional. Attacker sits in sandbox while real employee notices.

3

AI-Driven Defense — Behavioral Analytics

A different kind of AI
▶

The attacker's AI produces the disguise. The defender's AI catches the behavior the disguise can't hide.

How it works

The ML model learns what "normal" looks like per account — login times, devices, locations, typical first actions. After the MGM reset, the credential logged in from a new device, new IP, and immediately accessed admin functions the account had never touched. UEBA flags this in minutes. SOAR auto-remediates — revoke, quarantine, alert.

Takeaway
🔴 Attacker — Generative AI

Creates things that don't exist. Cloned voices, deepfake faces. Gave attackers scale, speed, and realism.

🔵 Defender — Analytical AI

Detects things that shouldn't exist. Anomalous logins, behavioral deviations. Detects by behavior, not appearance.

Defense in depth: physical factors + procedural policies + behavioral AI. No silver bullet — multiple layers, each catching what the previous one missed.

That's the arms race at the identity layer — and it's happening right now.
